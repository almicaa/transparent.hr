In Croatia, a pregnant worker is entitled to one paid day off a month for prenatal checkups. This right has existed since 2011. I didn't know about it. Neither did my employer.

During my pregnancy I went to regular checkups like every pregnant woman. I'd wait in the morning at the Petrova hospital to have blood drawn for antibody tests, then rush to work and spend days making up the hours I'd lost on tests. It occurred to nobody that there was a special, paid day off for exactly this. Not to me, not to the people in accounting, not to my managers.

And that day doesn't have to be one whole day. It can be two hours one morning for a blood test, then two hours off early another day for an ultrasound, and so on through the month, up to a full working day, that is, 8 hours. All of those hours are excused and paid; you just agree the schedule with your employer.

I found out about this right by chance, when I no longer needed it.

## What exactly the law says

The right is set out in the [Maternity and Parental Benefits Act](https://www.zakon.hr/z/214/zakon-o-rodiljnim-i-roditeljskim-potporama) (in Croatian), not in the Labour Act. Maybe that's why most people looking up "workers' rights" miss it.

- **How much:** one working day off a month for prenatal checkups ([Roda](https://www.roda.hr/media/attachments/udruga/programi/pravo/roditeljska_i_rodiljna_prava.pdf)).
- **Pay:** that day counts as time spent at work, and the employer pays it as a regular working day.
- **Since when:** the right was introduced by amendments to the law in [2011](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2011_03_34_767.html). The new act in force since 1 January 2023 kept it.

## How to use it

1. **Give notice in writing.** You tell your employer in writing that you're using the right, two working days before the scheduled checkup ([Unija](https://unija.com/hr/prava-trudnih-radnica/)). A short email with the date of the checkup is enough.
2. **Split the day into hours if that suits you better.** In agreement with your employer, you can spread that day's hours over several appointments in the month ([Roda](https://www.roda.hr/media/attachments/udruga/programi/pravo/roditeljska_i_rodiljna_prava.pdf)). Useful if you have two shorter checkups instead of one long one.
3. **Don't use up your annual leave.** You don't have to take annual leave or make up the hours for checkups.

If your employer doesn't know about this right, which is very likely, send them a [link to the law](https://www.zakon.hr/z/214/zakon-o-rodiljnim-i-roditeljskim-potporama). It isn't a matter of goodwill; it's a legal obligation.

## Why nobody knows about it

I'm not the exception. Back in 2017 the parents' association Roda warned that many employed pregnant women don't know about this right ([Roda](https://www.roda.hr/udruga/dokumentacijski-centar/reakcije/analiza-izmijenjenog-i-dopunjenog-zakona-o-rodiljnim-i-roditeljskim-potporama.html)). Nine years later, nothing much has changed.

The reasons are fairly simple:

- **The right is in the "wrong" law.** Anyone looking for workers' rights opens the Labour Act. This right is in the Maternity and Parental Benefits Act.
- **Nobody has to tell you.** Your gynaecologist doesn't ask where you work. Your employer doesn't have to offer a right they don't even know about. The state doesn't send a notice when you get your pregnancy record book.
- **Pregnant women don't ask.** When you're pregnant, the last thing you want is to seem demanding at work. It's easier to take annual leave and not make waves.

The result is a right that exists on paper but in practice is used only by those who happen to stumble upon it.

## Where to find out your rights as a worker

You don't need a lawyer to know your basic rights. You need to know where to look.

| What you need | Where |
|---|---|
| The text of the law, in the version in force today | [zakon.hr](https://www.zakon.hr) (consolidated texts) or [Narodne novine](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr) (the Official Gazette) |
| Pregnancy and parental rights, explained simply | [HZZO – maternity and parental benefits](https://hzzo.hr/rodiljne-i-roditeljske-potpore), the handbook ["Imam pravo"](https://www.cesi.hr/uploads/document/attachment/235/Imam-pravo-2023__1_.pdf) (CESI), an [overview of rights](https://www.roda.hr/media/attachments/udruga/programi/pravo/roditeljska_i_rodiljna_prava.pdf) (Roda) |
| The rules at your employer | Work regulations and the collective agreement, if there are any. Ask HR or your union representative |
| Advice for your specific situation | A trade union, associations for workers' and parents' rights, free legal aid |
| Reporting a breach of your rights | The [State Inspectorate](https://dirh.gov.hr) (labour inspection); for discrimination on grounds of pregnancy, the [Gender Equality Ombudsperson](https://www.prs.hr) |

All of these sources are in Croatian. One tip: when you read a law on a website, check the date of the consolidated text. Laws change, and old articles stay online for years.

## You can't use a right you don't know about

Transparent.hr started because of salaries. Job ads without a salary make candidates spend hours on hiring processes without knowing whether it's even worth it. But the problem is wider than pay. Workers in Croatia don't know how much they'll earn, and often they don't know what they're entitled to either.

Knowing your rights shouldn't depend on luck or on whether you have a friend who's a lawyer. If you're pregnant, or know someone who is, pass this article on. If you're an employer, check whether your HR knows about this right.
