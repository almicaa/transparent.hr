In October we wrote that the pay transparency deadline had [passed in silence](/en/blog/fiscalisation-vs-pay-transparency). The silence is over. On 2 November 2026 the Ministry of Labour presented draft changes to the Labour Act under which an employer would have to tell a candidate the starting salary or its range **before the job interview, or at the latest during it**.

Since then the headlines have been flying: "Salaries will no longer be a secret", "You'll know your pay before you're hired", "The pay gap can't exceed 5%". Some of it is true, some of it is exaggerated, and some of it simply isn't written anywhere.

We read the sources and separated what we know from what's being guessed.

> Important: this is a proposal. The draft is in public consultation until 2 December 2026 and doesn't apply yet.

## What's certain

This is what the ministry presented directly ([HRT](https://vijesti.hrt.hr/hrvatska/predstavljene-izmjene-zakona-o-radu-koje-donose-vecu-transparentnost-placa-12989032), [mirovina.hr](https://www.mirovina.hr/novosti/vijesti/88543/izmjene-zakona-o-radu-podatak-o-placi-obavezan-prije-ili-tijekom-razgovora-za-posao/vijest)):

- **Salary before or during the interview.** Every candidate must be told the starting salary or salary range. Anita Zirdum of the ministry's labour directorate put it this way: it will no longer happen that you "do the interview and almost get to signing the employment contract" and only then someone tells you the salary, "and maybe you turned down some other job" ([ICV](https://www.icv.hr/2026/11/mijenja-se-zakon-o-radu-place-vise-nece-biti-tajna-a-stizu-i-nova-pravila-za-godisnji-odmor/)).
- **Average pay, not your colleagues' pay.** Workers can ask for the average pay for their category of work, broken down by gender. They still won't find out what a particular colleague earns.
- **An end to secrecy clauses.** Contract terms that forbid workers from talking about their pay would become void, without every contract having to be changed ([Lika-Express](https://www.lika-express.hr/gospodarstvo/transparentnost-placa-izmjene-zakona-o-radu-2026)).
- **Job evaluation.** All employers would have to evaluate jobs by skills, responsibility, effort and working conditions, in a gender-neutral way. Pay differences remain allowed where they are objectively justified.
- **Reports from larger employers.** Employers with more than 100 workers would report on the gender pay gap. The data would go through a National Pay Transparency System set up by Fina (the state financial agency), at an estimated cost of around €750,000.
- **Penalties and a transition period.** An employer who doesn't provide the required information commits an offence. The amounts of the fines haven't been presented. At least six months have been announced for employers to adapt after the law takes effect.

## What's being said, and what we actually know

| Claim | What we actually know |
|---|---|
| "The salary will have to be in the job ad." | **Not according to the draft.** What was presented is "before or during the interview". The Directive mentions the job ad as an example but doesn't make it mandatory. |
| "Employers will no longer be allowed to ask about your previous salary." | **The Directive forbids it** (Art. 5). It wasn't mentioned when the Croatian draft was presented, so check the final text. |
| "Your colleagues' salaries will be public." | **No.** Only averages for the same category of work, by gender, are available. |
| "The pay gap between men and women can't exceed 5%." | **No.** 5% is the threshold above which larger employers must carry out a joint pay assessment, if they can't objectively justify the gap and don't fix it within six months (Art. 10 of the Directive). Justified differences are still allowed. |
| "The new rules apply from February 2027." | **An announcement, not a fact.** The date hasn't been confirmed, and with six months to adapt, the obligations realistically start later. |
| "Employers must reply within two months." | **That's the deadline in the Directive** (Art. 7). The deadline in the Croatian draft hasn't been presented. |

## Who's for it and who's against

**Trade unions support the changes.** The Union of Autonomous Trade Unions of Croatia (SSSH) points out that the burden of proving a pay difference is justified would be on the employer ([financije.hr](https://financije.hr/izmjenama-zakona-o-radu-placa-ce-biti-poznata-vec-na-razgovoru-za-posao/)). Back in August, Dražen Jović of the Independent Trade Unions of Croatia warned of what we think too: "The law is one thing, its application another."

**Employers are worried.** In August, the president of the Croatian Employers' Association (HUP), Irena Weber, called the Directive a disproportionate administrative, legal and financial burden and said they would seek a temporary suspension of its application ([Kodex](https://kodex.hr/gospodarstvo/hrvatska/nove-izmjene-zakona-o-radu-razlike-u-placi-vise-nece-biti-tajne-sindikati-zadovoljni-iz-hup-a-zabrinuti/)). There's also a fear that managers won't be able to reward those who work harder.

We understand that concern, but the draft doesn't bear it out. Differences for experience, performance and responsibility remain allowed. They just have to be explainable.

## Our view: "during the interview" is too late

If you only learn the salary at the interview, you've already written a cover letter, taken time off, and maybe gone through a test and one round of selection. We once [spent seven hours](/en/blog/seven-hours-of-selection) that way before we heard the number.

The Directive gives a published job ad and information before the interview as examples. The Croatian draft chooses the softer option. Only a salary in the ad really saves time for both candidates and employers, because only those who are happy with the number apply.

> A salary range in a job ad costs nothing. An hour spent interviewing for a job you'd never accept does.

## When the rules could apply

| When | What | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 7 Jun 2026 | EU deadline for transposing the Directive expired. Croatia missed it | known |
| 2 Nov – 2 Dec 2026 | Public consultation on e-Savjetovanja | ongoing |
| 2 Dec 2026 | Deadline for the Platform Work Directive, which the same draft transposes | EU deadline |
| Dec 2026 – early 2027 | The government finalises the bill and sends it to Parliament | estimate |
| 8 days after publication in the Official Gazette | The law takes effect (general rule) | rule |
| + at least 6 months | End of the transition; employers' obligations realistically start only then | announced |
| 7 Jun 2027 | First pay gap reports for employers with 150 or more workers | Directive deadline |
| 7 Jun 2031 | First reports for employers with 100 to 149 workers | Directive deadline |

Croatia is not the exception. By the June deadline only a handful of member states had fully transposed the Directive ([DCI](https://blog.dciconsult.com/the-transposition-deadline-has-passed)). Until the changes are passed, the current Labour Act applies, and "salary negotiable" is still legal.

## In brief: annual leave and delivery workers

- **Annual leave.** Leave from the previous year can still be used until 30 June of the following year. Those who couldn't use it because of sick leave or parental rights would get until 31 December, and if they're prevented again, three more months: 15 months in total after the year the leave was earned.
- **Platform work.** Platforms would bear wider liability for the aggregators that employ delivery workers and drivers, for example when an aggregator doesn't pay wages.

## What you can do right now

### As candidates

- **Ask for the range before the interview.** It will soon be your right, and it's already a perfectly reasonable question.
- **You don't have to say what you've earned so far.** No law obliges you to. Say what you expect.

### As citizens

- **Comment on the draft** on [e-Savjetovanja](https://esavjetovanja.gov.hr) by 2 December 2026. The ministry has to respond to every comment. If you think the salary belongs in the job ad, now is the time to say so.

### As employers

- **Don't wait for the law.** Publish the range in your ad today. You'll get fewer applications, but better ones, and a six-month head start on those who adapt at the last minute.

## Transparent.hr isn't waiting for the law

On [transparent.hr](/en/jobs) we show only job ads that list the salary, collected from Posao.hr, MojPosao.hr and HZZ. For now they're a minority of ads. We hope the law soon makes us redundant.

Until then, start with the ads that tell you straight away what your work is worth.
