At the end of 2025, all of Croatia was in a rush. Tradespeople, flat-rate taxpayers, accountants, small firms and large companies were looking for an e-invoicing provider, comparing prices and learning what KPD codes were. The deadline was 1 January 2026, and everyone knew it couldn't be missed.

Months before the deadline, everyone also knew what it would cost to miss it: for some offences, fines reached €66,360 for companies and €6,630 for individuals. In the end the Tax Administration announced there would be no misdemeanour proceedings against those who cooperated. But by then it no longer mattered. Just three weeks after the deadline, more than 300,000 taxpayers were in the system.

**The threat of a fine was enough.**

At the same time, Croatia was supposed to introduce pay transparency rules by 7 June 2026. The deadline passed. The law wasn't passed, and changes to the Labour Act are only expected at the end of the year. Job ads without a salary are still the rule, not the exception.

## Two deadlines, two approaches

When the state needs something from its citizens, deadlines are firm. The law is passed, the platform is launched, and the fines are announced in advance so everyone knows what awaits them.

When it comes to citizens' rights, deadlines become more flexible. The law is "being drafted", "in its final stage", "expected soon". The deadline passes, and nothing happens.

Fiscalisation wasn't simple. It was a technical reform that affected hundreds of thousands of businesses, new infrastructure and dozens of e-invoicing providers. Pay transparency asks for far less: a salary range in the ad and the right of workers to know how pay is set.

> If the harder thing could be done, why can't the easier one?

## Why is that?

**The state is fastest when its own revenue is at stake.** Fiscalisation means less VAT fraud and more money in the budget. It was also funded with EU money from the National Recovery and Resilience Plan, and that money comes with deadlines and conditions.

**Not everyone has the same seat at the table.** Employers have their associations and a voice in drafting laws, and that's legitimate. Their concerns about paperwork and deadlines are real. But candidates have no such voice. Nobody represents the person who spent seven hours in a hiring process for a job whose salary they didn't know.

**The problem is invisible.** The Tax Administration sees every e-invoice in real time. Nobody tracks job ads without a salary. Nobody counts the hours candidates lose on processes for jobs they wouldn't have taken had they known the number. The cost is real, but it's spread across thousands of people.

**Delay has no clear price.** When a state misses a deadline for an EU directive, the European Commission can start proceedings, but that takes years. In the meantime nothing changes.

## How long will it stay this way?

As long as there's no clear public pressure and no clear penalties.

The law will probably be passed, because Croatia has to pass it. The question is what kind of law. Will it have clear penalties, or only principles? Will anyone actively enforce it, or will it wait for complaints?

> A law on paper and a law in practice are not the same thing.

## What can we do?

### As candidates

- Ask about the salary at the start of the process. It's a perfectly legitimate question.
- When you have a choice, favour ads that list the salary.
- When the law takes effect, find out what your rights are and use them.

### As citizens

- Follow the public consultation on [e-Savjetovanja](https://esavjetovanja.gov.hr). When the draft law is published, comment on it. The ministry has to respond to comments.
- Talk about pay openly. The more normal the topic, the greater the pressure.

### As employers

- Don't wait for the law. Put a salary range in your ad today. You'll get fewer applications, but better ones.
- Every employer who publishes the salary shows others that it can be done and that it's not a problem.

## What the new law should include

- **Clear, enforceable penalties**, just as clear as with fiscalisation.
- **A duty to put the salary in the ad itself**, not "somewhere before the interview". That's the only way to really save time for both candidates and employers.
