"Salary negotiable." Anyone who has looked for a job in Croatia knows the phrase. The question is how common it really is. Is it the exception or the rule?

Throughout autumn 2026, we counted job ads every day on three sources: posao.hr, MojPosao and HZZ (the Croatian Employment Service). The answer is clear. **The salary is the exception, not the rule.**

## The main finding

| Job site | Share of ads with a salary | Range of daily measurements |
|---|---|---|
| posao.hr | 4.6% | 4.1–5.8% |
| MojPosao | 12.2% | 4.8–14.4% |
| HZZ | 5.2% | 0.5–8.7% |

On posao.hr and HZZ, roughly one ad in twenty lists the salary. MojPosao does somewhat better, but even there almost nine ads out of ten say nothing about money.

HZZ surprised us most. It's a public service, and yet 95% of its ads give no salary figure, even though its job posting form has a dedicated "Gross salary" field.

## Where it's worst: sectors

For the breakdown by sector and city we used only HZZ, because it's the only source for which we also have the ads without a salary.

| Sector | Ads | With salary | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | 661 | 91 | 13.8% |
| Transport | 1,054 | 130 | 12.3% |
| Tourism and hospitality | 2,369 | 173 | 7.3% |
| Mechanical engineering and shipbuilding | 1,167 | 29 | 2.5% |
| Construction and surveying | 1,250 | 21 | 1.7% |
| Electrical engineering | 513 | 8 | 1.6% |
| Healthcare and social care | 2,607 | 38 | 1.5% |
| Administration | 1,687 | 11 | 0.7% |
| Education and science | 3,463 | 1 | 0.03% |

**Education and science is the largest category on HZZ, and out of 3,463 ads exactly one lists the salary.** Healthcare and administration aren't much better. Interestingly, pay in these fields is largely set by fixed coefficients and isn't a secret, yet it still doesn't appear in the ads.

The most open are sales, transport and hospitality: jobs where workers are hard to find and pay is often the main selling point.

## Cities

The differences between cities are smaller than those between sectors. In no city does even one ad in ten list the salary.

The best are Zadar (6.9%) and Pula (6.7%), probably because of their larger share of tourism jobs. They're followed by Zagreb (5.4%), Šibenik (5.1%) and Čakovec (4.6%). Rijeka, Split, Osijek and Varaždin are between 3 and 4%.

At the bottom is Slavonski Brod with 0.9%, fewer than one ad with a salary per hundred.

## When the salary is listed, how much is it?

Across 1,274 ads with a monthly salary in Croatia, the median is **€1,165**. Construction offers the most (median €2,000), followed by installation and maintenance (€1,700). At the bottom are transport, agriculture and pharmacy at around €1,050, which is exactly the 2026 minimum gross wage.

A word of caution: most of these ads come from HZZ, where the amount is always gross, while some from MojPosao give net pay. So the figures show what employers who publish the salary offer, not the average salary in Croatia.

## A fixed amount or a range?

Five out of six ads with a salary (83%) give a fixed amount; the rest give a range.

A range is only useful if it's reasonable. The median width of a range is €400, so the upper limit is typically about 30% higher than the lower one. That's perfectly usable.

But there are extremes. The widest range we found was €500 to €5,000, an upper limit ten times the lower one. Next came €1,600 to €6,000 and €1,500 to €4,500.

> A range of €500 to €5,000 is technically a listed salary. In practice it's "negotiable" in another form.

## Gross or net?

Another problem: 12% of ads with a salary don't say whether the amount is gross or net. The difference is large. A candidate who sees "€1,500" doesn't know whether they'll get €1,500 or about €1,100 take-home.

## What's changing?

EU Pay Transparency Directive 2023/970 requires employers to tell candidates the starting salary or range in advance. The deadline for transposing it into national law expired on 7 June 2026. Croatia didn't make it, and [changes to the Labour Act](/en/blog/salary-before-the-interview-labour-act-changes) are expected at the start of 2027.

Once they take effect, the figures in this analysis should change. We want to see how fast and by how much. We'll keep measuring, and you can follow the trend on the [Analytics](/en/analytics) page.

## How we measured

- **Share with a salary:** daily counts of ads on the job sites themselves. MojPosao from 4 July, posao.hr from 24 September, HZZ from 11 October to 10 November 2026.
- **Other analyses:** 18,385 ads collected from 8 September to 10 November 2026. The same ad posted for several branches of the same employer counts as one.
- **Limitations:** for HZZ we count the new ads of the day, not all active ones. The medians mix gross and net amounts. Categories are mapped from the names each job site uses, so they aren't fully comparable. Only aggregate data is shown, with no individual employers.

## Conclusion

The numbers are clear: a salary in a job ad is still the exception. For almost every ad, candidates apply blind and only learn the amount at the interview, if then.
